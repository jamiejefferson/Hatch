OptionalWorks with Jev

Hatch works with Jev from TypeSafe.ai.

36/36

Jev picked the right element every time in our test, in a median of 0.8 seconds.

Jev is a small model built to make fast, inexpensive, structured decisions that software can use directly. Hatch asks it which element your agent means, so your agent says “the button that refuses cookies” and Hatch presses it.

Your agent can also hand Jev a whole goal, such as “search for "blue badge" and open the first result”. Jev chooses each click and keystroke, Hatch carries it out, and your agent reads a record of every step before it carries on. Your agent keeps the decisions and Jev takes the repetition. Stage 11 shows how a run works.

By hand

snapshotclicksnapshotfillsnapshotpress_keysnapshotclickone turn of your agent for every call

With Jev

jev_run {goal}one turn of your agent for the whole run

Connect it in Settings with your own key, from console.typesafe.ai/settings/keys or from openrouter.ai/keys.

  1. Settings“Connect Jev” sits near the foot.
  2. →
  3. Save and connectPaste your key, then press this.
  • Jev stays off until you connect it, and “Disconnect Jev” switches it off again.
  • Each use sends the page’s outline to TypeSafe, through OpenRouter when the key is an OpenRouter key. A page where Hatch filled a sign-in is never sent.
  • The Activity panel lists each run with its steps, Jev’s confidence in each one and what the run cost.
  • Below 80% certainty Hatch does nothing and lists the closest elements.
  • To try it, connect Jev, then ask your agent to press “the sample button in stage 4”.

11

Let Jev take the repetitive steps.

A search, a long list or a checkout asks your agent for the same small decision again and again: read the page, choose the next click, wait, read the page again. Each of those is a full turn of a large model. With Jev connected, your agent states the goal once and Jev chooses each step.

Hatch asks Jev three things at every step: which action comes next, whether the goal is reached, and whether the run is stuck. Jev answers all three in one reply and writes no text, which keeps each step short. TypeSafe charges $0.042 for a million tokens read, and every run reports what it cost.

4/4

We gave Jev four goals on four public sites and it finished all four. On GOV.UK it refused the cookies, searched for “blue badge” and opened the right result: five steps in 3.0 seconds, for $0.0012.

Your agent sets the goal

“Search for "espresso machine" and open the first result under £300.” Any text Jev must type sits inside quotes, because Jev chooses and never writes.

Jev takes the steps

It clicks, types, chooses from dropdowns, presses Enter and scrolls. It stops when the goal is reached, when it is stuck, or when it runs out of steps or time.

Your agent reads the record

Every step comes back with Jev’s confidence and what the page did, beside the final page as an outline. Your agent checks the result and decides what happens next.

You see what it cost

The Activity panel shows each run as one line, such as “done (8 steps, $0.0031, 4.2s)”, and opens to show every step.

Jev suits mechanical goals. Your agent still does the reasoning: which product to pick, whether a result is right, what to try when a page surprises it.

To switch it on, open Settings, paste your TypeSafe or OpenRouter key under “Connect Jev” and press “Save and connect”. Then point your agent at the Agent page, which tells it when a goal suits Jev.
